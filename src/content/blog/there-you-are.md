I faved this photo when I took it. Something happened in my brain like a puzzle piece sliding into place. I wanted to mentally put it into my two main categories, autism or bipolar. I've spent a decade detangling the mania of bipolar and only recently the autism.

But it was perhaps the secret third option: my self worth.

I look at the photo and I recall the ease of my body riding a bike in the early evening warmth of July. A muscle tee, a sports bra and jean shorts. Nothing exciting or new, so what was the puzzle piece feeling? Maybe it was observing my body in and from a neutral stance. No forced self love or attaching my worth to something I'll attain down the road.

No more sitting ducks. No more waiting.

Neutrality is such a balanced and welcoming friend that holds up no mirror or judgement. It's a big breath before the scaries.. except the scary part never arrives.

It does bring up another question though. If hating yourself can't result a positive outcome and neither can forced self love... what's the difference here? My autism brain likes the black and white aspect. 1+1 should equal 2 so why is it just as detrimental to my brain? Its two radical sides of the same coin, and forcefully doing anything is never going to work.

I assume this is an obvious statement but it's news to me and my late diagnosed autism brain. I'm perhaps a little embarrassed by this. The embarrassment of course stems from always having to perform or mask my autism in such way that I go unnoticed, or not cause issues at the very least.

But maybe I have stopped maintaining every single person's opinion of me. The goal now is to actively disappointed people I wouldn't trust with my safety. Within it is also to be misunderstood - my absolute worst fear as an autistic person because there is some trauma for sure.

I aim to be a cosmic inconvenience for mediocre folk. It's a post-people-pleaser path, and it's rocky.

I guess, be who you needed as a kid.
